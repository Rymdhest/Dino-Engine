# Halls

The buildings that can be walked into. There are three, all in the Haven: **the Forge**, with a crafting bench; **the Gaming House**, an old barn with a gaming table in the middle of its threshing floor; and **the Archive**, where what a player's heroes have done stands on displays, one record each, that speak when someone walks up to them. The bench and the table are placeholders: what they do is still to be decided, and using one says so. Read this before changing a hall, adding one, or touching anything that has to know about rooms: the cutaway, light, the floor, wildlife, villagers or the records.

## What a hall is

- **A design, and nothing stored.** A hall is a `HallDesign` (`World/WorldHall.cs`): a floor plan in its own frame, with its front towards +z, its size, its walls' thickness and height, its doors and windows (`HallOpening`), what stands in it (`HallPiece`), what stands round it outside, where its keeper stands and what's used in it (`HallSpot`), the Archive's displays (`HallExhibit`) and the places villagers walk to in it (`Visits`). The three are written in `World/Content/HallDesigns.cs`, which is the only hand-written data. A `WorldHall` is one standing in a world, with its centre, its yaw and its floor's height, and `ToWorld` and `ToLocal` go between its frame and the world's.
- **Two models.** Everything about how a hall looks is grown from its design by `Modelling/Procedural/Architecture/Halls/` (headless, so it builds on worker threads and in the labs): `HallForge.Build` makes its shell (walls, roof, chimneys, dormers, its doors and windows, and whatever stands round it outside) and its inside (floor, furniture, panelling, candles, books, everything in it). Their ids are `hall.forge` and `hall.forge.inside`, and `WorldAssetLibrary` builds them like any other structure. Each hall is drawn from its own palette (`HallKit`), and no hall wears black iron, oxblood or brass, which are the Reich's (`LORE.md`). A piece is built in its own frame, its middle on the floor and its front towards +z, and says where its lights and flames are in that frame too: `Hall.At` with the list of lights puts them where its shapes go. Without that every candle on a piece burned in the middle of its hall, and the Gaming House's fire under its table, which is how a person found it.
- **Wound to face out.** A hall's cut draws whatever is seen from behind as the face of a section, flat, dark and unlit by its own glow (`hallCapped`), and `MeshBuilder`'s beams and tubes were always wound the wrong way round, which nothing else shows. So both of a hall's models are built with `MeshBuilder.Outward`, and anything made of loose faces goes through `Hall.Face`, `Hall.Tri` or `Hall.Board`, which wind towards the side they're told. A board is a `Hall.Board`: its grain runs along it whichever way it lies, where a box's runs up its sides.
- **Placed like anything built.** `SiteBuilder.PlaceHall` checks the ground, levels a pad for it and keeps its doorsteps clear. Its walls are solid runs broken at its doors (`HallDesign.WallRuns`), and every piece marked solid inside or out is a footprint, so `Passable`, the pathfinder and `CharacterMover` know a hall the way they know anything else: what looks solid is solid, and a door is the only way in.
- **The ground under a floor is exactly the floor.** People stand on the height map, which is drawn 0.15 m higher (`TerrainRenderer.SurfaceOffset`). So a floor is laid 3 cm over where people stand (`Hall.FloorTop`), clear of the ground the sun's shadows are drawn from, and the drawn ground is cut out of every room (`underHallFloor` in `Shaders/Halls.glsl`, in `Terrain.frag`). Only a door's sill stands over the ground in its doorway (`Hall.SillTop`). `TerrainBakeStage.LevelHalls` levels the ground under every hall to its floor last of all, as a street's shoulder, stamped after the pads, once lifted a corner of the Archive 9 cm through its floor.

## The Gaming House

A person called the first one a complete disaster: one wood on everything, hard to get about in, and its fire under the table. It's an old barn now, turned to games of chance, and laid out as a barn is (`HallDesigns.GamingHouse`, built by `GamingHouse.Pieces.cs` and `GamingHouse.Barn.cs`, with the props it shares with the Forge in `HallProps.cs`).

- **Three bays.** The great doors are in the middle of its long side and open onto the threshing floor, flagged in stone, which runs straight through between four posts (`HallDesigns.GamingBay`, `GamingPostDepth`) under two tie beams. The great table stands in the middle of it under a cart wheel of candles, with one place at its front kept empty, where a hero stands; its dealer is behind it and the bar behind them both, against the back wall. The bay on the left is for sitting: the hearth, a settle and a chair, the hayloft in its far corner over the house's tuns with a ladder up, and a stage by the door. The bay on the right keeps its stalls, which are booths, and the wheel of fortune hangs at its far end.
- **Clear to walk.** Everything stands against a wall or between the posts, so the threshing floor and the aisle either side of it are open, and nothing leaves a gap that looks wide enough and isn't: a gap is either clearly shut or 2.5 m. The ground under the loft is filled by its store (`PieceKind.LoftStore`), as anyone under it would be hidden. Keepers and the places villagers visit need a metre of ground clear all round them on a metre's grid (`Passable`), so the way behind the bar is over three metres wide.
- **For this camera.** It looks down from the south, so a tall back is on the north side of whatever it's the back of (the settle, the benches in the booths), what's on a table matters more than what's under it, and anything hung is seen 0.42 m further north for every metre it hangs above the floor: the wheel of candles is hung low and a little towards the door, so that it lies over the cloth and not over the dealer. Nothing stands above `WallTop`, as it would go with the roof.
- **More than one wood.** The floor is flags, boards and the hearth's stone (laid by the shell, `HallShells.Floor`, to `GamingHouse.HearthFloor`). The walls are rubble at the foot, weathered boards, each its own grey, and lime between dark timbers over them. The frame is hewn timber (`bark-bare`), furniture is `timber`, casks and floors are `wood`, the chimney is brick, and the colour is cloth in the Haven's teal, saffron, blue, cream and blossom. Hay is a plain gold with its stalks laid on it, as thatch reads as a piece of roof.
- **Its light.** The wheel of candles, the fire, a lantern on each post and each stall's heel post, one under the loft and little ones on the bar and in the two booths furthest from a lantern: 17 lights, of which the fire, the wheel and the lanterns cast shadows. What only doubled another was taken out, and it costs what the old one did (11.9 ms a frame at 3440 x 1369, standing at the table).

## The Forge

A person asked for the most intense smith's house there is: metal, leather and cloth under one roof, steam, smoke and fire, alive, and every detail telling something. It's laid out by `HallDesigns.Forge` and built by `Smithy.Metal.cs` (the fire's trades), `Smithy.Soft.cs` (leather and cloth) and `Smithy.Works.cs` (what hangs on the walls and lies underfoot), with its hearth, which is part of the building, in `HallShells.ForgeHearth`. What it shares with the Gaming House, mugs, lanterns, casks, sacks and the palette, is `HallProps.cs`.

- **A line and two sides.** The fire is at the back in the middle, open to the room under a copper hood, with the great bellows beside it, and straight down the room from it are the anvil, the smith, and the master's bench, which is the crafting bench and faces the door. On the left of whoever comes in (the plan's -x) is the hot side: the smelting furnace in the far corner with charcoal, ore and bar stock beside it, metal cooling in its moulds along the wall, the quench trough, the grindstone, and by the door what's finished, what's waiting and what nobody came back for. On the right is the soft side: a cauldron and two vats along the back under cloth hung to dry, a hide in its frame and one over the beam, the leather table under its windows, a saddle, and by the door the loom, the spinning wheel, the tailor's table, a dress form and the cloth. Everything stands against a wall or on the line from the door to the fire, so there's a way round the room on either side, each over three metres wide.
- **Three work here.** The smith stands behind the bench, between it and the anvil, and two more of the Haven keep the leather and the cloth (three `SpawnRole.Keeper` spots). A keeper faces the middle of the room, so each stands where that's towards whoever comes in.
- **What each thing says.** Every piece is in the middle of something, and none of it is written anywhere but in how it's built. Three bars lie in the coals, each as hot as how long it's been there. Ingots cool down the casting bed from white to grey, and the last mould was only just poured. A blade stands at every stage along the grindstone's rack: a blank black from the fire, ground, polished, hilted. One suit of harness is finished and the one beside it came back from a land clawed open, its shield bitten through. A shelf holds what was mended for Defenders who never came for it, each with its tag, and someone keeps candles lit under it. The hide in the frame is a raptor's, with the rent that killed it stitched shut. The banner on the tailor's table has three of its five petals sewn. A ballista for the Haven's wall stands half built on its trestles, one arm in its skein and the other waiting, and a cat is asleep on the cloth. The master's bench holds what a Defender brings to it: a sword, a drawing, a tooth. Keep this up in anything added: a thing that's only there is clutter.
- **For this camera.** The copper hood is set high (its mouth at 6.4 m) and well back, as anything hung over the fire lower than that hides it from a camera that looks down from the south. Tall things, the furnace, the hide's frame, the loom, stand at the back and the sides. The world is at about twice real scale and a person is 4.7 m, so a machine drawn at the size it would be carried at is a toy: the ballista is built half as big again (`b.Stretch`).
- **More than one surface.** The inner walls are stone, as nobody panels a forge. The floor is flags where it's hot, boards where cloth is cut and cobbles between (`Smithy.HotFloor`, `BoardFloor`, laid by `HallShells.Floor`), with a kerb between them. Metal is steel, bright where it's been ground; the hood is copper; the furnace is firebrick banded in iron; leather goes from rawhide to tan to dark; and the colour is cloth in the Haven's dyes. Nothing in it is black iron, oxblood or brass (`LORE.md`).
- **Alive.** A model can hold a lasting effect as it holds a light or a flame: `AssetLight.Emitting(at, "Smithing.Quench", scale, forward, tint)` names a recipe in the catalogue, and `AssetLightEntities` plays it there for as long as the model's streamed. The Forge's are `Particles/Content/Smithing.cs`: `Coals` on the hearth (flames, sparks, a little smoke up into the hood), `Furnace` at the smelter's mouth, `Quench` and `Vat` for steam, `Mould` over the newest ingots, `Drip` under each drying cloth in that cloth's own dye, and `Cinders` over the hot floor. Whatever plays indoors is short-lived, so it's gone before the roof, and hardly moved by the wind, which doesn't blow in a room. `Dino WorldLab halls` fails a model naming an effect the catalogue doesn't have.
- **Its light.** The hearth's fire and the furnace's cast shadows, and lanterns light each bench: 12 lights, 7 flames and 10 effects inside. A light within a metre and a half of a pale or glowing surface washes it white, and so does a glow over about 1.3, which is why the hearth's light is in front of and above its bed, the furnace's is out towards the room and not in its smoke, and the coals glow no harder than they do. Pale flat patches on a floor (ash, lime, damp, sand) read from above as sheets of paper or plate, so the floor keeps only dark scorch, coal dust and spilt dye.
- **What it costs.** 0.35 ms a frame more than the forge before it at 3440 x 1369, standing at the bench (10.84 to 11.20 ms, three runs of each, turn about): its lights, two more people and its particles.

## The Archive

A person asked for it clean and elegant, perfectly clear to view what's kept in it, crisp, and majestic, almost a great cathedral. It's a hall of marble laid out as a church is (`HallDesigns.Archive`, built by `Basilica.Floor.cs`, `Basilica.Order.cs`, `Basilica.Pieces.cs` and `Exhibits.cs`).

- **A nave and two aisles.** The door opens on a nave that runs between two rows of three marble columns (`HallDesigns.ArchiveArcade`, `ArchiveBays`) to a dais of three steps under the end wall, where the tyrant's skull stands as an altar would, under a great arch with the Haven's blossom in gold. The aisles either side are a pair: the same windows, the same banners, a ring of candles over each.
- **Nothing stands in front of anything.** The camera looks down from the south, so every record but the skull is against a wall with the floor open before it: two in each aisle against the end wall, facing down the hall, each under an arch of its own, and two against each side wall, between its windows, under a banner. What has a front is against the end wall, where it's seen whole (the board of trophies, the split stone, the treasury, the stone for the fallen); what's the same from every side or lies open to the sky is on the side walls (the hourglass, the chart, the climb, the hoard). Only columns, benches and candles stand in the open, and a column's line is kept clear to the north of it, as that's what it hides.
- **Seen as the camera sees.** A thing is mostly seen from above, so what it's known by has to face up and south. The skull lies on its side on a tray of dark velvet tilted towards whoever stands before it, so that it's seen from beside, the eye, the holes round it and every tooth: upright it was a long white wedge. The hourglass hangs in a cradle tipped far out across the view, as upright, or tipped towards the camera, it's the plate on its top. The treasury is steps open to the sky and never a case with a lid. A record's light is set well out in front of it and over it.
- **The floor is most of the picture** (`Basilica.Floor`, laid by the shell): slabs of two whites, each cut from its own block (its own place and turn in the marble, so no vein runs on from one to the next), a dot of teal where four meet, a band round the walls, a way of teal up the nave, a great compass with the blossom at its heart where the nave opens before the dais, a ring under every column, and a round of teal before every record, where whoever reads it stands. Everything let into it is a few millimetres over the last (`Basilica.Inlay`, whose marble lies as the world does: a face made corner by corner starts its texture again at each, which on a round of wedges is a kaleidoscope).
- **Marble** is a material of its own (`MaterialCatalog.Marble`, `TEXTURES.md`), pale, with which marble it is in the vertices: white, ivory, teal, a rose for the blossom and a gold for every line. It's honed and not mirrored: as smooth as water, every light over the floor showed in it as a white blot. Walnut, dark velvet, and gold on what's turned or cast are the rest; no black iron, oxblood or brass (`LORE.md`). The plaster that was on its walls had stains and patches fallen away, which is why they're marble too.
- **Where the roof is taken off.** A column ends in its capital and the foot of the vault it carried, under where the walls end. Nothing in the room is high enough to light the top of it, so each has a little light of its own over it, or it ends in a black round after dark; that light has to be under where the walls end, or it's taken to be outside the room and lights nothing in it (`insideHall`).
- **Its light.** Three rings of candles on chains, one over the nave inside the door and one over each aisle, hung where they lie over open floor as the camera sees them, which is a little north of where they hang; tall stands of candles at the corners of the dais and inside the door; a candle either side of every column; and one soft light to each record. 25 lights and 88 flames, of which the three rings cast shadows.
- **Kept.** It's the one hall where nothing is in the middle of anything: books are in sets, a shelf to a binding, in two tall cases in the end wall and one in each corner by the door; the keeper's desk has its ledgers squared; and nothing lies on the floor.

## Seeing in

- **The cutaway.** When the hero stands in a hall, its roof and the walls facing the camera go, thinning out over a third of a second, for the camera alone (`HallCutawaySystem`, `Shaders/Halls.glsl`, `Model.frag`). The sun's shadows and global illumination still see the whole building, so light comes in by its windows and doors as it would. It opens a little inside the doorway and closes a little outside it, so a hero standing in a door doesn't flicker it. The back of whatever the cut opens is drawn as the cut's own face, flat and lit from above (`hallCapped`). Up to eight halls near the camera are given to the shaders (`HallShaderData`, in the globals).
- **What's above the cut goes with it.** A chimney's smoke over an open hall fades out (`Particle.vert`) and so does anything flying over it (`Model.frag`'s wildlife), or a bird crossing above the room would read as one inside it.
- **An inside is only drawn when it can be seen.** The geometry pass writes coverage and discards, so nothing under a roof is rejected early: `HallCutawaySystem` hides a hall's inside (`glModel.Hidden`) unless it's open or the hero is within 9 m of it, from the camera and from shadows alike.

## Light

- **Light stays in its room.** A candle lights its room and a lantern in the street doesn't light through the wall (`hallsLetThrough`, in `Spot_Light.frag`, and the same rule as bits a hall in `Point_Light.frag`, `hallRoomsOf` against `HallShaderData.RoomsOf`), and a light inside a room big enough to be one still casts shadows (`LocalShadowCasters` skips only what a light sits inside that's under 6 m). Global illumination keeps light out of a room by the same rules that keep the sun off a wall's far side (`GI.md`): halls are the first rooms it has had.
- **What glows shines.** Candles, sconces, chandeliers, a forge's fire and the lanterns by the doors are `AssetLight`s built with the models, streamed with them, and bounced by global illumination.
- **A room reads.** A camera can't adapt the way an eye does, so inside a room the exposure rises (`RenderEngine.Adaptation`, eased in and out with the cutaway) and the room gives back a share of the sky and sun that come in by its windows, warmed by the wood and lime it falls on (`hallRoomLight`). It's worked out from the sky and sun, so with them out the frame is still black. The flat sky that stands in where global illumination has no answer is mostly shut out of a room (`hallShelter`).

## What lives in and round them

- **Keepers.** Each hall has someone who keeps it, a villager who stands at their post (`SpawnRole.Keeper`, `VillagerComponent.Keeps`), looks at whoever comes near and back to their work after, and says what a smith, a dealer or an archivist would (`VillagerSystem`'s `Keeping`, read `LORE.md`).
- **Visitors.** Nearly half the walks Haven villagers take are into one of the halls, to one of its `Visits`, by the way in through its door: the exact pathfinder, pulled straight wherever there's a clear line, and given up if they stop getting anywhere for 5 s.
- **Nothing wild goes in.** Nothing living stands, feeds or scuttles within 1.2 m of a hall's walls (`LifeLand.HallBerth`), leaves that fall over a room are gone, and anything flying keeps over a roof from 3 m past its eaves (`LifeLand.Overhead`), up to the hall's `Top`, which `Dino WorldLab halls` holds to its model. No grass grows under a hall or close round its walls (`TerrainSurface.HallGround`).

## The game's side

- **Opening them.** Halls are meant to be earned, one at a time, but how isn't decided, so for now every hall is open to every hero (`Halls.AllOpen`). `HeroState.HallsOpen` is where opened halls will be kept, by name, and `Halls.IsOpen` is what both ends ask before anything in one can be used: using something in a shut hall says it isn't open yet. Nothing yet stops anyone walking into one: that needs its door, and a way to show it, when it's decided.
- **What's used.** The crafting bench and the gaming table are `InteractKind.Workbench` and `GamingTable`, with marks like anything else usable (`UseMarks`). The gaming table opens a page that says it isn't built yet (`UI/HallPanels.cs`).
- **The crafting bench** opens the bench in a window on the left, beside the hero's own panel on the right (`UI/CraftingBench.cs`, `HeroPanel.OpenBench`). It does one thing so far, fusing (`Progression/Crafting.cs`): two Magic things of the same sort (the same `ItemSlot`, a weapon of the same kind, a Page of the same kind) become one Rare, the first's base, look, level and contents with its prefix and the second's, then its suffix and the second's, for `Crafting.FuseGoldPerLevel` gold a level of the first. The second is used up, and has to hold nothing. Two with the same line fuse, and what's made has it twice, as two lines that both count, which `ItemGenerator.Fix` and `FolioAffixes.Fix` keep (nothing rolls an affix twice). A line the first couldn't roll doesn't fuse, so what's made is always within the rules and loads as it was. Nothing moves onto the bench: its two places only say which cells of the pack they show, which stay there, faint, and what couldn't go with what's on it is dimmed. What's made goes in the first's cell. It's a change to what's carried, so it goes through `AskGear` (`HeroAction.Fuse`), made at once on the client by the same rules and answered with the truth, and only in the Haven. `Dino BuildLab check` holds the rules (`CraftingChecks`), `Dino NetLab crafting` both ends, and `--panel bench --carry 36` opens it in the game with the first pair that fuses on it.
- **The records.** `HeroRecords` (`Progression/Records.cs`) is what a hero has done: foes and tyrants slain, the hardest blow, falls, the swiftest land freed and its level, gold gathered and every unique found. The server keeps it as things happen (`Combat.Credit`, the blow path, `ServerSession.HeroFell` and `Judge`, `GroundLoot.Take`, gold), it goes to the client with the rest of the hero (`Protocol.WriteHeroSummary`, the uniques as a bit each by their place in the catalogue) and it's saved with them. A swiftest land is only timed from arriving to freeing in one go: not after a fall, not restored from a save and never in the Haven. What can't be true is brought into line on loading (`HeroRecords.Sanitize`).
- **The Archive's displays.** Nine records, each on a display of its own (`ExhibitKind`, built by `Exhibits.cs`). While the hero's in the Archive every display has its name and its number over it, small, so that the whole of what's kept is read from the door; walk within 7.5 m of one and that opens into its card, fading in and out, the nearest only (`UI/ArchivePlaques.cs`). A card hangs over its display's far edge, which from a camera that looks north is clear of it, and with the camera drawn in too close for that it's a caption over the action bar, as pushed down from the top of the screen it lay across the display itself. A tag is only drawn over a display that's in the picture. Each card says the record of all the player's heroes together, the best where it's a best, with whose it is, and the hero being played's own beneath. The others are read from their saves, and only when a hero was chosen from the menu, so a scripted run reads nobody's but its own.

## Adding a hall

A new `HallKind` goes on the end. Write its design in `HallDesigns`, its shell in `HallShells` and its inside in `HallInteriors` (and any new piece in a file of the hall's own, as `Smithy.*.cs`, `GamingHouse.*.cs` and `Basilica.*.cs` are, or in `HallFurniture` if halls share it), and stand it in `HavenBuilder` on one of the plots kept for it (`HavenBuilder.Plots`), facing south: a hall's front is the wall that comes down for the camera, which only looks north. Anything used in it needs an `InteractKind` on the end, a line in `Halls.Of`, and its lines in `UseMarks`. Its keeper needs lines in `Keeping`. Give it a `Top` its model doesn't pass.

## Checks

- `Dino WorldLab check --seed 1610 --count 1 --archetype haven` holds every hall (`WorldValidator.CheckHalls`): one of each, both models drawn where it stands, nothing else built or grown in it, level dry ground under its floor with no road through it and room to stand in, ground round its walls that their footing reaches, every door walked through from the village into the room over ground that meets its sill, walls that stop anyone everywhere but the doors, its keeper and what's used in it where they were put, every place villagers visit reachable, and every display near enough to read. It's been seen to fail with a wall's collision taken away, a corner of a floor lifted and a visit beside a candle stand. Halls anywhere but the Haven fail it.
- `Dino WorldLab halls` builds every hall's models with no GPU and fails one over 120,000 triangles outside, 260,000 inside or 40 lights, one naming an effect the particle catalogue doesn't have, or whose model passes its `Top` or falls 3 m short of it.
- `Dino LifeLab giants --seed N` on several seeds, as the village is most of the isle and the giants live round it.
- Shaders only compile when the game runs: look at its console.

## Where it stands

**Nobody has walked into one but the bots and the stills.** The Gaming House was rebuilt as a barn after a person called it a disaster, and they haven't seen it since: ask first whether it has the old barn they wanted, whether it's easy to get about in, and whether the beams and the wheel of candles get in the way of seeing. Its outside is as it was, a timber-framed house with a porch, and not yet a barn. The Forge was rebuilt the same day as a smith's, a tanner's and a tailor's in one, and nobody has seen it either: ask whether it's as intense as they wanted, whether the steam, smoke and fire read as alive or as noise when they move, whether the stories in it can be read from the game's camera, and whether three people working there is right. It has no sounds of its own yet (hammer, bellows, hiss). Its outside is as it was too. The Archive was rebuilt the same day as a hall of marble laid out as a church, and nobody has seen it: ask whether it's as clean and as grand as they wanted, whether every record reads at a glance, whether the skull on its side is right for the end of the nave, whether the names over the displays help or are in the way, and whether marble that's honed and not mirrored is slick enough. Its floor is the first polished surface in the game, and nobody has watched what it shows while walking. What it costs wasn't measured cleanly, as a game was being played on the machine at the time: about a third of a millisecond more than the old one at 3440 x 1369 with both slowed alike, on its lights and on what its floor reflects. Ask how the halls look inside and out from the game's camera, whether the cutaway reads as opening the building or as a glitch, whether the rooms are lit as they should be, whether the Archive's cards are where they should be and say what they should, and whether the village reads as a little town. How the table and the unlocking work is still to be decided. Fusing at the bench has never been tried by a person: ask whether its price and the window feel right.
