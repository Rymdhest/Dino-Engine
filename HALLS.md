# Halls

The buildings that can be walked into. There are three, all in the Haven: **the Forge**, with a crafting bench; **the Gaming House**, with a gaming table; and **the Archive**, where what a player's heroes have done stands on displays, one record each, that speak when someone walks up to them. The bench and the table are placeholders: what they do is still to be decided, and using one says so. Read this before changing a hall, adding one, or touching anything that has to know about rooms: the cutaway, light, the floor, wildlife, villagers or the records.

## What a hall is

- **A design, and nothing stored.** A hall is a `HallDesign` (`World/WorldHall.cs`): a floor plan in its own frame, with its front towards +z, its size, its walls' thickness and height, its doors and windows (`HallOpening`), what stands in it (`HallPiece`), what stands round it outside, where its keeper stands and what's used in it (`HallSpot`), the Archive's displays (`HallExhibit`) and the places villagers walk to in it (`Visits`). The three are written in `World/Content/HallDesigns.cs`, which is the only hand-written data. A `WorldHall` is one standing in a world, with its centre, its yaw and its floor's height, and `ToWorld` and `ToLocal` go between its frame and the world's.
- **Two models.** Everything about how a hall looks is grown from its design by `Modelling/Procedural/Architecture/Halls/` (headless, so it builds on worker threads and in the labs): `HallForge.Build` makes its shell (walls, roof, chimneys, dormers, its doors and windows, and whatever stands round it outside) and its inside (floor, furniture, panelling, candles, books, everything in it). Their ids are `hall.forge` and `hall.forge.inside`, and `WorldAssetLibrary` builds them like any other structure. Each hall is drawn from its own palette (`HallKit`), and no hall wears black iron, oxblood or brass, which are the Reich's (`LORE.md`).
- **Placed like anything built.** `SiteBuilder.PlaceHall` checks the ground, levels a pad for it and keeps its doorsteps clear. Its walls are solid runs broken at its doors (`HallDesign.WallRuns`), and every piece marked solid inside or out is a footprint, so `Passable`, the pathfinder and `CharacterMover` know a hall the way they know anything else: what looks solid is solid, and a door is the only way in.
- **The ground under a floor is exactly the floor.** People stand on the height map, which is drawn 0.15 m higher (`TerrainRenderer.SurfaceOffset`). So a floor is laid 3 cm over where people stand (`Hall.FloorTop`), clear of the ground the sun's shadows are drawn from, and the drawn ground is cut out of every room (`underHallFloor` in `Shaders/Halls.glsl`, in `Terrain.frag`). Only a door's sill stands over the ground in its doorway (`Hall.SillTop`). `TerrainBakeStage.LevelHalls` levels the ground under every hall to its floor last of all, as a street's shoulder, stamped after the pads, once lifted a corner of the Archive 9 cm through its floor.

## Seeing in

- **The cutaway.** When the hero stands in a hall, its roof and the walls facing the camera go, thinning out over a third of a second, for the camera alone (`HallCutawaySystem`, `Shaders/Halls.glsl`, `Model.frag`). The sun's shadows and global illumination still see the whole building, so light comes in by its windows and doors as it would. It opens a little inside the doorway and closes a little outside it, so a hero standing in a door doesn't flicker it. The back of whatever the cut opens is drawn as the cut's own face, flat and lit from above (`hallCapped`). Up to eight halls near the camera are given to the shaders (`HallShaderData`, in the globals).
- **What's above the cut goes with it.** A chimney's smoke over an open hall fades out (`Particle.vert`) and so does anything flying over it (`Model.frag`'s wildlife), or a bird crossing above the room would read as one inside it.
- **An inside is only drawn when it can be seen.** The geometry pass writes coverage and discards, so nothing under a roof is rejected early: `HallCutawaySystem` hides a hall's inside (`glModel.Hidden`) unless it's open or the hero is within 9 m of it, from the camera and from shadows alike.

## Light

- **Light stays in its room.** A candle lights its room and a lantern in the street doesn't light through the wall (`hallsLetThrough`, in `Point_Light.frag` and `Spot_Light.frag`), and a light inside a room big enough to be one still casts shadows (`LocalShadowCasters` skips only what a light sits inside that's under 6 m). Global illumination keeps light out of a room by the same rules that keep the sun off a wall's far side (`GI.md`): halls are the first rooms it has had.
- **What glows shines.** Candles, sconces, chandeliers, a forge's fire and the lanterns by the doors are `AssetLight`s built with the models, streamed with them, and bounced by global illumination.
- **A room reads.** A camera can't adapt the way an eye does, so inside a room the exposure rises (`RenderEngine.Adaptation`, eased in and out with the cutaway) and the room gives back a share of the sky and sun that come in by its windows, warmed by the wood and lime it falls on (`hallRoomLight`). It's worked out from the sky and sun, so with them out the frame is still black. The flat sky that stands in where global illumination has no answer is mostly shut out of a room (`hallShelter`).

## What lives in and round them

- **Keepers.** Each hall has someone who keeps it, a villager who stands at their post (`SpawnRole.Keeper`, `VillagerComponent.Keeps`), looks at whoever comes near and back to their work after, and says what a smith, a dealer or an archivist would (`VillagerSystem`'s `Keeping`, read `LORE.md`).
- **Visitors.** Nearly half the walks Haven villagers take are into one of the halls, to one of its `Visits`, by the way in through its door: the exact pathfinder, pulled straight wherever there's a clear line, and given up if they stop getting anywhere for 5 s.
- **Nothing wild goes in.** Nothing living stands, feeds or scuttles within 1.2 m of a hall's walls (`LifeLand.HallBerth`), leaves that fall over a room are gone, and anything flying keeps over a roof from 3 m past its eaves (`LifeLand.Overhead`), up to the hall's `Top`, which `Dino WorldLab halls` holds to its model. No grass grows under a hall or close round its walls (`TerrainSurface.HallGround`).

## The game's side

- **Opening them.** Halls are meant to be earned, one at a time, but how isn't decided, so for now every hall is open to every hero (`Halls.AllOpen`). `HeroState.HallsOpen` is where opened halls will be kept, by name, and `Halls.IsOpen` is what both ends ask before anything in one can be used: using something in a shut hall says it isn't open yet. Nothing yet stops anyone walking into one: that needs its door, and a way to show it, when it's decided.
- **What's used.** The crafting bench and the gaming table are `InteractKind.Workbench` and `GamingTable`, with marks like anything else usable (`UseMarks`), and each opens a page that says what it's for and that it isn't built yet (`UI/HallPanels.cs`).
- **The records.** `HeroRecords` (`Progression/Records.cs`) is what a hero has done: foes and tyrants slain, the hardest blow, falls, the swiftest land freed and its level, gold gathered and every unique found. The server keeps it as things happen (`Combat.Credit`, the blow path, `ServerSession.HeroFell` and `Judge`, `GroundLoot.Take`, gold), it goes to the client with the rest of the hero (`Protocol.WriteHeroSummary`, the uniques as a bit each by their place in the catalogue) and it's saved with them. A swiftest land is only timed from arriving to freeing in one go: not after a fall, not restored from a save and never in the Haven. What can't be true is brought into line on loading (`HeroRecords.Sanitize`).
- **The Archive's displays.** Nine records, each on a display of its own (`ExhibitKind`, built by `Exhibits.cs`): walk within 7.5 m of one and its card rises over it, fading in and out, the nearest only (`UI/ArchivePlaques.cs`). Each card says the record of all the player's heroes together, the best where it's a best, with whose it is, and the hero being played's own beneath. The others are read from their saves, and only when a hero was chosen from the menu, so a scripted run reads nobody's but its own.

## Adding a hall

A new `HallKind` goes on the end. Write its design in `HallDesigns`, its shell in `HallShells` and its inside in `HallInteriors` (and any new piece in `HallFurniture`), and stand it in `HavenBuilder` on one of the plots kept for it (`HavenBuilder.Plots`), facing south: a hall's front is the wall that comes down for the camera, which only looks north. Anything used in it needs an `InteractKind` on the end, a line in `Halls.Of`, and its lines in `UseMarks`. Its keeper needs lines in `Keeping`. Give it a `Top` its model doesn't pass.

## Checks

- `Dino WorldLab check --seed 1610 --count 1 --archetype haven` holds every hall (`WorldValidator.CheckHalls`): one of each, both models drawn where it stands, nothing else built or grown in it, level dry ground under its floor with no road through it and room to stand in, ground round its walls that their footing reaches, every door walked through from the village into the room over ground that meets its sill, walls that stop anyone everywhere but the doors, its keeper and what's used in it where they were put, every place villagers visit reachable, and every display near enough to read. It's been seen to fail with a wall's collision taken away, a corner of a floor lifted and a visit beside a candle stand. Halls anywhere but the Haven fail it.
- `Dino WorldLab halls` builds every hall's models with no GPU and fails one over 120,000 triangles outside, 260,000 inside or 40 lights, or whose model passes its `Top` or falls 3 m short of it.
- `Dino LifeLab giants --seed N` on several seeds, as the village is most of the isle and the giants live round it.
- Shaders only compile when the game runs: look at its console.

## Seeing it

`--haven true` with `--at x,z`. The square is about `--at 211,142` (its middle is a bed with a tree in it, `SQUARE.md`) and the walk up to the gate `--at 211,155`. The Archive is entered at `--at 165,156` and seen from outside at `--at 165,138`; by its Foes Slain display at `--at 171,157`. The Gaming House is `--at 252,161` and `--at 252,141`, the Forge `--at 172,104` and `--at 172,85`, and the training ground `--at 207,68`. `--render gi=0` shows what global illumination brings in, and `--render sun=0,sky=0,lights=0,emission=0` has to be black, inside a hall as anywhere.

## Where it stands

**Nobody has walked into one but the bots and the stills.** Ask first how the halls look inside and out from the game's camera, whether the cutaway reads as opening the building or as a glitch, whether the rooms are lit as they should be, whether the Archive's cards are where they should be and say what they should, and whether the village reads as a little town. How the bench, the table and the unlocking work is still to be decided.
