# The main menu

The first thing anyone sees: a real place in a real world, with a hero standing in it, and as little laid over it as there can be. Read this before changing it.

## Heroes

A hero is one save file in `heroes/` beside the other saves, with all the world they were in, so nothing about one touches another (`HeroRoster`). Whoever was played last comes first. `SaveGame.PathOverride` is what points the rest of the game at the chosen one, so nothing else knows there's more than one.

The save from before there were several is moved in as the first hero, called Wanderer. It's copied first and only then moved aside to `save.json.before-heroes`, so there's no moment at which it's nowhere. Testers carry their saves across builds, so that has to keep working.

A hero who's deleted is moved to `heroes/deleted/`, as the game can't undo it and a slip of the hand shouldn't be final. A save that can't be read isn't shown, and isn't touched.

Someone new has no land yet (`SaveGame.NeverPlayed`). Their first step grows one, and they get the prologue.

**At the menu nobody is being played.** `_hero` is null and `Save` does nothing, as whoever's on the spot is only being looked at, and from the moment "Choose another hero" is pressed there's nowhere safe to write to.

## The place

`TitleScene` is a world like any other, drawn by everything that draws one, with no server and no session. Anything a client runs has to cope with `GameSession.Current` being null. The hero is one body, on the bones and in the gear they have in the game (`Wardrobe.Dressed`), standing at their ease by `CharacterAnimationSystem`, which needs nothing but a position.

**The picture is settled: don't change it unless a person asks.** On 2026-11-12 a person asked for it once and for all, after the old way kept moving it: lush grass, water, trees, a mountain far off and the giants, after a shot of sauropods across a lake from a meadow, with the hero in front, their back to the camera. Before that it was a spot searched for in a seed's land, and every change to how lands grow moved it, the last time onto a keep's paving. On 2026-11-15 they asked that the same picture be a filmic one, the most important shot in the game as it's the first anyone sees: fog in the distance about the lake and the giants, trees on to the far back so that nobody can see where the terrain ends, a more dramatic sun, and above all depth, the giants looking a long way off. How that's done is The shot, below.

So it's a world of its own kind, `vista` (`ArchetypeCatalog.Vista`, never drawn from a seed), laid out by `World/Vista.cs` rather than drawn: only its noise comes from `TitleScene.Seed`. The hero stands on a meadow 8.5 m over a lake, the camera behind them looking north as the game's does, and the meadow falls away from their feet steeply and then more gently to the shore, as ground that fell gently first would be a brow and the long grass on it a horizon hiding the water. The lake is 150 m across the way it's looked over, and on its far shore, 250 m from the camera, is a level meadow where the giants graze (`Vista.Herd`), to the left of where the camera looks, so that none is ever behind the hero. Then woods everywhere, two ridges behind them, one behind the other (`Vista.Ridges`), and a mountain 700 m off with low peaks behind it along the north edge, raised by `MountainStage` as any land's rock is. Its one place is `SiteKind.Overlook`, where the hero stands, with nothing built; it has one biome (Wildwood), no roads, camps or packs, and its light is first light (`WorldMood`'s `Composed`, which no seed draws), the sun low ahead of the camera and to its right. Nothing taller than grass grows between the camera and the water (`Vista.InSight`), as shrubs and lone trees are scattered by chance, and one came up in front of the giants. `WorldValidator` checks what the picture needs of it (`--archetype vista --count 1`).

Terrain is a square of `ChunkRadius` chunks a side (22 here) whose middle is `ChunkAhead` (168 m) in front of the camera (`TerrainQuadTreeSingleton.Ahead`), as nothing behind it is seen: that reaches the far meadow and into the woods behind it. The lake has to lie inside it, as water is only drawn over terrain. No square can be wider than 22, as every chunk alive has a layer of the terrain's arrays and there are 2048 of them, the most NVIDIA's cards allow (`TerrainQuadTreeSingleton.MostRadius`); a square of 26 round the camera once handed out layers that didn't exist, and the land entered after the menu was drawn with its chunks' textures crossed, in squares. What's further is `World/FarLand.cs`: the world's own heights as plain meshes in 128 m tiles, rock darker on its faces and green on its ledges, the ground under its woods, and the forest that stands on it (The far forest, below). It's grown on another thread the first time the picture's shown, kept for the game, and meets the chunks at the world's own heights, with nothing inside their square, as anything there would be carved out of the grass.

**The Haven's giants are in the picture**, on the far meadow, between the menu and the hero; `FindHerd` gives them `Vista.Herd` and keeps them inside the picture's edges and no nearer than 55 m (`GiantShow`, in `WILDLIFE.md`). They're grown the moment the game starts, and take three seconds, so they're there from the first frame on any machine with a few cores, and thin in otherwise. What's heard here is heard from the camera, which is where their calls carry to. `FindSpot` and `FindHerd` still search any other world given with `--archetype`, for looking at others.

They look away from the camera, out over the land. That's what third person is, it shows what they have on, and heroes have no faces, as the game's own camera has never needed one.

`ChunkRadius` is raised here, as this view is nearly level and the game's never is. The weather's held clear, as a first look at a game shouldn't depend on the clock. The camera drifts a little, as a still picture looks like a held breath. `Engine.RenderEngine.context.focusPoint` is cleared every frame, or depth of field goes on focusing on wherever a game left it.

## The shot

What makes it a picture is depth, and every part of this is there to say how far off something is. It follows what painters and cameramen do: a dark, sharp foreground, a middle ground with something in it to measure by, and a distance that's paler the further it goes; shapes one in front of another; light from in front and to one side, which a face-on sun never gives; and a small figure before a large land. Everything here is the title's own and off everywhere else: the game's camera looks down on fifty metres, where none of it would show. `TitleScene` holds it every frame (`HoldTheAir`, `HoldTheWeather`), as a change of quality sets some of it again.

**The sun** is 28 degrees to the right of where the camera looks and 12.5 up (`Vista.SunAzimuth`, `SunHeight`): past the mountain's right shoulder, over the ridges, inside the picture on any screen and away from the menu's words. So the picture is seen against the light. Grass and leaves glow with it coming through them, everything has a lit edge, shadows come towards the eye, and the mist shines. It's brighter than a sun that low is (`WorldMood.Composed`), as a camera would be opened up for a view that's mostly in shade. **The ridges and the sun go together**: a ridge's shadow is five times its height at this angle, and the first ridge stands where its shadow ends at the trees behind the giants. Raise one, bring it nearer or lower the sun, and the giants graze in shade and the mist round them goes grey, with nothing failing but `Dino WorldLab check --archetype vista`, which marches from the hero and the giants' meadow towards the sun.

**Shadows that far** are a second map of the sun's, 800 m across (`TitleScene.FarShadows`, `GraphicsPresets.SunShadow`): the game's own ends a hundred metres ahead of this camera, so nothing across the lake cast or took a shadow. A wide map's offsets are scaled to keep its casters as far off in metres as the game's are (`DirectionalCascadingShadowComponent`), it's drawn every third frame (`Shadow.interval`), no grass is drawn into it, and a pixel well inside the fine map never asks it (`CASCADE_EDGE` in `SunShadow.glsl`).

**The air** is the volumetric fog, in two parts. The mist is its marched layer, laid level on the water (`TerrainFollow` 0, `BaseHeight` the lake's) and not on the ground: it lies over the lake and the far meadow, about the giants' legs, lit from behind and cut by the shadows of the trees the sun comes through, and the hero's meadow stands clear of it, so nothing near the eye is veiled. Its billows are 30 m across, as they're seen from 250 m, and `NoiseScale` is set once and never changed. The haze is the fog's distance haze given what a far view needs (`HazeHeight`, `HazeHorizon`, `HazeMost` on `VolumetricFogSingleton`, all nothing by default): it takes the colour of the sky along the horizon behind whatever it's in front of, read from the sky map, where it used to be the sky's one colour, a fifth as bright as the sky drawn. So each ridge is paler than the one before it, the mountain goes into the sky it stands against, and all of it warms towards the sun. `HazeMost` keeps the mountain from going altogether.

**The sky** has a low sun's glow (`WeatherSingleton.HorizonGlow`, `lowSunAir` in `Sky_Map.frag`): gold along the horizon towards the sun and round it, in place of the sky's blue and not added to it, as added it came to white, and the sky overhead deeper for it. The sun's own glare and rays are turned down (`CelestialBodyComponent.Glare`), as they fall off slowly and were a veil over half the picture. There are no clouds: the sky map is made for reflections and its clouds are blocks when looked at this low, and they put the land in shade.

**The lake** reflects the far shore, the giants and the mountain (`WaterSingleton.SeaReflections`; it's the sea of its world, which otherwise reflects only the sky). A ray off it is walked along the screen a few pixels at a time (`waterMarchAlong` in `WaterCommon.glsl`), as steps through the world long enough to reach the far shore came out in blocks, and what it finds is hazed by how far it went, as the copy of the scene it reads has no air in it yet. **The ripples never bend that ray.** Seen this low, a slope of half a degree moves what's reflected by twenty pixels, so a ray off each pixel's own slope cut the giants' reflection into slices that jumped as the ripples passed, and a person watching it said it glitched and looked unnatural. Still water seen along is a mirror drawn out up and down, so one ray goes off the water as if it were calm, and what it finds is spread up and down the screen by the slopes the ripples have (`waterReflectionAlong`, `WaterSingleton.SeaReflectionSpread`), which here is still water's and not the wind's, as the wind's leaves nothing of the giants. The ripples still show, in how much of the sky each one reflects. **The ray meets only what it went behind in its last few pixels** (`REFLECTION_DEPTH`, a share of the screen's height), never something held to a depth in metres: a ray off water further out than a bird's reflection reaches the bird's place on the screen already behind it, by more the further out it started, so metres enough for the far trees drew every bird crossing the lake down into the water as a zigzag line a fifth of the screen long, which a person noticed. Judge any change to it moving, from frames taken a moment apart: a still looked fine with the fault in it. `--film folder` works with `--menu true` as it does in a land, `--lifelog file` beside it says when birds cross, and two runs film the same moments, so frames from before and after a change can be laid side by side.

**The far forest** is what hides where the terrain ends. Past where the world's trees are streamed there used to be a canopy laid over the ground, a smooth green sheet, and a person saw where it began. Now there are trees (`FarLand.Forest`): every tree the world has out there and more between them wherever its woods are thick, some 26,000, each a picture of its model turned to the eye (`FarForestComponent`, drawn by `FarForestSystem` through the imposter renderer, which the game has off) and never the model, as a camera that drifts would have trees at a changeover distance flicker between the two. A kind's picture is a few thousand small draws, so they're taken one kind a frame once the far land is grown and kept by model (`TitleScene._pictures`), and the forest stands a second or so after the land under it. **It's in forced perspective**: from where the world's trees stop, they're smaller the further off they stand, down to 0.42 of their size at the mountain's foot, and closer together by as much, and none stands on bare rock. With full-grown trees to its top the mountain was a wooded knoll, which is what a 130 m hill in a 1300 m land is; with small ones at its foot and none above, it's a mountain, and the ridges are further off than they are.

**The lens** is stopped right down (`RenderEngine.LandscapeLens`): only the grass within 8 m of it is soft. With nothing to focus on, depth of field used to blur everything by its distance, which smeared the far land the air should have been softening.

**What it costs**, at 3440 x 1369 on a 7900 XTX with the pass timers on: 13.7 ms of GPU a frame against 12.6 before, 71 frames a second against 74. `--menu true --heroes folder --perf file` measures it.

Judged from stills at 16:9 and 21:9. Nobody has watched it move.

## The screen

Text on the picture, and a shade that fades to nothing well before the hero. Nothing has a box round it but what's being typed in. The menu's root is `HitTestMode.PassThrough`: `None` looks the same and stops the mouse reaching anything under it.

The game's name at the top is its logo, grown in code as everything else is and never imported (`UI/Logo.cs`): the mark, the tyrant posed by the game's own animator part way through a roar and cut to facets on a hill before the land's mountains, with one Defender on it, and the title set with the engine's own type, all a flat print in the land's red, bone and ink. It takes a second or two, so it's begun as the game starts (`Logo.Begin`, from `ArpgGame.Begin`) and the menu draws it once it's grown. `"Dino Defenders.exe" --logo folder` prints it with no window, the mark at every size a social page takes and the title over nothing, and `Marketing/Social/make_social.py` lays the banners from those, so a change to it in the game is a change everywhere.

## The pages

Everything that isn't the game itself is one thing (`UI/MenuKit.cs`): words on a scrim, a rail of them down the left at the same place as the main menu's, and rows of a name and a control. There's one size for each kind of text in `Kit`, gold for what's chosen and parchment for what isn't, and nothing has an edge but what takes typing or a key. A new page is a `MenuPage` with rows on it, and a new setting is a `MenuRow`. Don't reach for `Ui.Action` or a dressed panel here: that's what the old pause menu was.

`GameUi.Into` opens a page from wherever this is, and `Back` goes to that. The game's HUD, the marks on things and the labels on finds all go while a page is up (`GameUi.Clear`), as a page is all there should be to read.

## Settings

Everything takes effect at once and is kept at once, so nothing has to be confirmed. They're in `sound.json`, `video.json`, `interface.json` and `keys.json` beside the heroes (`SettingsStore`). A scripted run has the defaults and writes nothing, unless it's given `--settings folder`, so it can't read or change anyone's.

**Graphics quality is the only graphics setting**, on purpose, but for Global illumination, a toggle of its own beside it until it's been played enough to go into the presets (`VideoSettings.GlobalIllumination`, read `GI.md`). `GraphicsPresets` is what quality comes to, and what goes in it is what buys frames for what can't be seen from the game's camera. Measured at 3440x1369 on a 7900 XTX: High 120 FPS, Medium 177, Low 207, with pictures from the game's camera hard to tell apart. Most of it is multisampling and grass. A world's own settings go with the world, so `WorldScene.Enter` applies it again, and the sun's map is remade when it changes. Multisampling doesn't go below two: grass leans on it. Low is held to 120 frames a second at 1080p on a GTX 1660 Ti, measured in a window on screen: it draws the scene at two thirds of the window and sharpens it onto it (`RenderEngine.RenderScale`, `Shaders/Upscale.frag`, `--render scale=x,sharpen=y`), with the interface at the window's own size, and it has no contact shadows, ambient occlusion or bloom, shadows on four lamps, whose light reaches four fifths as far, and thinner grass that ends at 65 metres. A person chose those over cutting the scene's look further, as a render scale was the only thing that reached 120 with fog, lamps and shadows kept. A hidden run's frames take a few milliseconds longer than a window's, as its swap waits on the GPU: compare builds there by `GPU total` and the perf report's `GPU per frame` line (`Debugging/GpuFrameClock.cs`), never by its frame rate. It also says how many creatures and leaves a world can have about at once, though what they cost is hardly worth saving (see `WILDLIFE.md`). Another level is a value in `GraphicsQuality`, a line in `Presets` and a word on the page.

`DisplayMode` is `WindowHandler.ApplyDisplay`. Borderless is always the monitor's size, so Resolution has nothing to say there. A screen of its own is entered and left by hand, as OpenTK only remembers where a window was for what it did itself.

`InterfaceSettings` is how the interface reads, and whatever's in it is read as it's drawn, so a new one is a property and a row. Health, Mana and Barrier are on the action bar and can also be over the hero, which is on unless turned off. Both are `Hud.DrawPools`, so they can't come to disagree. Whether an attack is marked on the ground goes by the rarity of what's throwing it, a setting each (`InterfaceSettings.Marks`), and normal enemies start off, as a crowd of those is what hides what matters.

## Keys

Nothing reads a key but through `Bindings`, and nothing shows one but by asking it, so a change reaches everything at once: the bar, the mark on a thing that can be used, every hint and card. **Never write a key in a string, and never read `Keys` directly.** The server doesn't know what anyone's keys are, so it can't say them.

A key means one thing, so taking one swaps the two and nothing is ever left with none. Menu starts on Escape, which nobody else can have, so whatever it takes a key from moves to where it started, or else to a free key, and the page says where. Anything can be on the keyboard or the mouse, and what's on the mouse asks the interface first (`Bindings.Asked`), so a press on a window never swings or opens anything. The first two places on the bar repeat while held (`HeroRules.HeldSlots`), whichever key they're on, and the rest are presses.

Three things aren't anyone's: Escape always goes back, as someone has to be able to get out; F3 is the developer's; and Ctrl and F is the pack's search, by habit from everywhere else. Clicking in the interface is always left to pick and right to open. A new action is a value in `GameAction`, a default, and a row in `Bindings.Page`, which `check` holds.

## Testing

`Dino BuildLab check` holds the roster and the keys. Nothing else about this can be proved by a bot: post real mouse messages to a hidden run.

```
"Dino Defenders.exe" --menu true --heroes folder --hidden true --size 1720x960 --snap file --sound false
```

**Only the pause menu stops the world.** Playing alone, the game pauses for the pause menu and the pages reached from it or covering everything (settings, the glossary, travel with friends: `GameUi.OnPage`) and for nothing else: with the hero's panel, a book, the talent tree, the map or anything used in the world open, the hero still walks and fights, as a click that isn't on a window still reaches the world (`Gui.WantsMouse`). What's opened by using something closes once the hero is `GameUi.UseLeash` from where they used it.

`--quality low` runs at a level, for `--perf`, and `--panel settings` or `pause` opens a page. Exclusive fullscreen can't be tried beside someone playing: even a hidden window comes to the front for it.

**`--heroes` is not optional.** Without it a run with `--menu true` is working on that person's own heroes, and it moves their old save. Any other scripted run skips the menu, and `--seed N` shows the spot in another world.
