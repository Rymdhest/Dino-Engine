# Lore

What Dino Defenders is about, and the words, names, looks and sounds that say it. Read this before writing anything a player reads, naming anything, or adding an enemy, a place or a look. It's the main lore of the game, and everything else is there to mean it.

Status: written on 2026-11-08 with the dinosaurs. The game says it through each land's chronicle, what places are, what people say, and every enemy's name, look and voice. **Nobody has played it.** See "Where it stands" at the end.

## The one idea

**Dinosaurs are invading the land, and someone has to stop them.** They didn't come by themselves. An empire from over the sea, the Saurian Reich, grows them from blood found in amber, raises them from bones in the ground, puts them in harness and sends them ahead of its soldiers. Every land a hero sets foot in is one the Reich has taken, and freeing it means bringing down the tyrant that holds it.

It's **Jurassic Park meets Ghost of Tsushima**:

- **From Jurassic Park**: animals brought back from the deep past by people who think they can own them. Awe first, then dread. A big animal is heard and felt before it's seen. They're animals, not monsters: they hunt in packs, call to each other and test the fences, and what's horrifying is what people did to have them.
- **From Ghost of Tsushima**: an invasion from over the sea onto a land worth fighting for. One defender with the wind at their back, taking it back a place at a time and kindling the shrines the invaders put out. The land stays beautiful while the enemy holds it, as it's what's being fought for.

The Reich is fascist, coded on the Nazis: an ideology of blood and breeding, uniforms, ranks and numbered units, and people treated as stock. It's fictional through and through, and "Writing it" says where the line is.

## The story

Across the sea is an empire that believes the strong are owed the world, and that strength is in the blood. It calls itself the Saurian Reich. Its blood-doctors at the Amber Institute found the blood of the old beasts in amber and learned to grow them again, and the Reich made them its weapon: the raptor ahead of the soldier, the horned beast against the wall, and a tyrant at the head of every army.

It came over the water in iron ships. It took the coasts first, then the valleys and the islands, one land at a time, and it holds every one of them the same way:

- **A commander** holds it from its keep, with a **tyrant**: a war-beast bred for one officer and named in the Reich's tongue, such as Eisenmaul or Blutzahn. Commanders are never met. Their tyrants are what holds a land, and what its freedom depends on.
- **A brood** keeps it: a numbered unit of soldiers and beasts, such as the Seventh Talon or the Ninth Hatch. Its **pens** are where it keeps and breeds its animals between hunts, and its **war-bands** hold the roads.
- **The Ossuary** digs in the graves and under the old stones. It's the Reich's other order, and it raises what the amber can't bring back, straight out of the rock, with a cold light where the eyes and the heart were. What the Institute fails to grow, it keeps going with iron and bile.
- **The people** pay the tithe: grain, and sons for the pens. They bar their doors when the roaring starts, and have stopped waiting for anyone to come.

Not everything the Reich brought stays in harness. Raptors, spitters and ravagers that got out of the pens breed in the deep woods now, and skitters swarm wherever anything has died, which is everywhere the Reich has been.

**The hero is a Defender.** Nobody sent them. They come ashore at the Landing, and the people call them what they call anyone who stands between them and the teeth. They free a land the way it was taken, a place at a time: they burn out the pens, put what the Ossuary raised back in the ground, rekindle the shrines, and bring down the tyrant at the keep. Then the wind turns to guide them home, and the next land they go to is a little deeper into what the Reich has taken and a little harder.

The Reich came by sea. The Defenders go by the waygates, the land's own old roads, raised by no people anyone remembers, and every one of those roads comes first to **the Haven**: a small isle of blossom in the last light of the day, and the one place the Reich has never found. It has the only great beasts the Reich never took, a few **giants** older than anyone's memory, who browse the tops of its trees and are nobody's. Defenders rest there between lands, and choose the next at the map table, where every land the great waygate knows is drawn in light. What they take from the Reich comes with them: its **orders**, sealed in oxblood wax, which say where a brood is bound and what it's bringing, and which, given to the map table, open the way onto exactly that.

Where the Reich has held a land long enough, it digs an **arena** into a hill: a bowl of stone stands round a floor of sand, with gates at the foot of the stands where the brood's handlers wait. The people of the land are marched in on fight days and counted going in, made to watch the commander's tyrant feed on whoever's thrown to it, so that nobody forgets whose land it is. A Defender who gets that far walks down the ramp to it. When the fight goes against the tyrant, the handlers throw up the gates and let in the rest of the brood. The crowd is silent, and wants the Defender to win, and only says so under its breath.

In the lands the Reich holds, the villages that are left are **hiding**. They keep a waygate, a shrine and a little trade going behind barred doors, and they let in anyone who isn't the Reich.

Up to five can share a land, and they're the Dino Defenders.

## The Saurian Reich

**What it believes.** That the strong are owed the world, that strength is in the blood, and that blood can be bred. Everything else follows: people are stock, sons go to the pens, and an animal is whatever it can be made into. It names everything it owns in its own tongue, numbers whatever it breeds, and puts order above everything.

**How it's ordered.**

| Who | Called | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A commander | A rank and a family name. The ranks are plain army words: Kommandant, Oberst, Hauptmann, and Brutmeister, its master of a brood | Oberst Grauvogt |
| A brood | An ordinal and a word: Brood, Talon, Legion, Hatch or Fang | The Ninth Hatch |
| A tyrant | A compound in the Reich's tongue | Eisenmaul (iron maw), Donnerfuss (thunder foot) |
| Its soldiers | Greycoats: legionaries with spears, marksmen with bows, and wranglers in oxblood leather, who drive the beasts with goads | Reich Wrangler |

**What it looks like.** Three colours that nothing else in the game wears: **black iron, oxblood and brass**. Greycoats wear charcoal with oxblood trim under iron helms. Its beasts wear its harness: an iron collar with its bolts and oxblood straps round the chest and belly, and on the big ones an iron mask over the snout, spikes, and a plate on the back with a pole carrying its pennant. Their hides carry its war paint, an oxblood band over the snout and a ring round the tail. From across a field, a player should tell a war-beast from a wild one by colour alone.

**Its mark** is the skull of the first tyrant the Amber Institute ever raised, in profile, in brass on oxblood, and it flies on the pennants of the great beasts. It looks like nothing but what it is, which matters: the first mark was a curved claw on red, and from across a field it read as a crescent on a red flag, which is a real country's.

**Its voice** is the tyrant's roar, heard at night from the keep. It has no anthem, and no music of its own yet.

## The Amber Institute

The blood-doctors. They found blood in amber and grew beasts from it, and they didn't stop at what the amber held: the spitter's venom is theirs, which no fossil ever said it had, and so are the hot blood, the tough hides and the tempers. Every living dinosaur in the lands is theirs or bred from theirs: only the Haven's giants aren't, as the Reich has never found them. They number their hatches and keep their failures, which go to the Ossuary.

## The Ossuary

The Reich's order of the dead. It digs where the land keeps its bones, in churchyards and barrows and under standing stones, and raises what it finds.

Its work is in two kinds, and a player should tell them apart at a glance:

- **Bones**: skeletons raised straight out of the rock, the old beasts the amber can't bring back. Bare bone with earth in the hollows, and a cold cyan-green light in the eyes and where the heart was, which is all that moves them. These are the skeleton dinosaurs.
- **The rotting**: the Institute's failures, animals that hatched dying or were raised before the blood took, kept on their feet by an iron collar, a glass of glowing bile on the back and brass tubes into the neck. Their hide has gone grey and green and split where it rotted through, with ribs showing in the worst of it, and there's a sick yellow-green light in their eyes. These are the zombie dinosaurs. They're dumped where the land is wet enough to take them, so there are more of them in marshes.

Where it digs, the land's own dead don't lie quiet either: the restless dead, the grave knights and the mourners are the land's, stirred up. It's always night in a churchyard.

## The beasts

Every dinosaur is grown from a species and a seed, so no two are alike: `DINOSAURS.md` says how. Each kind is after real animals, bent towards what reads from the game's camera, and away from anything one film made famous.

| Kind | After | What it is | Where it's met |
|---|---|---|---|
| War Raptor | Deinonychus | The Reich's hounds, in harness, and most of any war-band. A killing claw held up on each foot, and often quills down the back | On the roads and around the pens, ahead of the soldiers |
| Raptor | | Raptors that got out, breeding wild | The deep woods |
| Skitter | Compsognathus | Little scavengers that come in dozens | Swarming, in the wild |
| Spitter, Wild Spitter | Dilophosaurus | Thin crests along the snout and a bright pouch under the throat. Spits the Institute's venom from behind the pack | Behind war-bands, and wild |
| Ravager, Wild Ravager | Allosaurus and Carnotaurus | A heavy-skulled killer with horns over the eyes. What leads a war-band when raptors won't do | Leading packs |
| Hornback | Triceratops and its kin, smaller and meaner | Beak, horns and frill. Driven at walls, and walls go | Leading war-bands |
| Tyrant | Tyrannosaurus | A commander's war-beast | At the keep: every land's boss |
| Bone Tyrant, Bone Raptor, Bone Ravager | | The Ossuary's skeletons | Among the dead, and now and then a land's boss |
| Rotting Tyrant, Rotting Raptor, Rotting Hornback | | The Ossuary's failures | Among the dead in wet country, and now and then a land's boss |
| Reich Legionary, Reich Marksman, Reich Wrangler | | The greycoats | With the war-bands, and on guard over what the Reich has taken |
| Giant | Brachiosaurus and Giraffatitan | The Haven's own, and free: long-necked and gentle, cropping the tops of trees | The Haven, and behind the main menu. Never in a land, and never fought |

**Every land's boss is a tyrant**, and it's the strongest thing in the game. Seven in ten are bred and harnessed, and the rest are a bone tyrant the Ossuary raised or a rotting one, half each (`Chronicle.BossKindOf`). The chronicle names it and the keep holds it, so the story and the fight are always the same animal.

The land has enemies of its own, and they're part of the same story:

- **Outlaws** are turncoats who sold the land to the Reich and work the roads beside it, about a quarter of what holds them.
- **The dead** are the land's, stirred up by the Ossuary's digging, with its bone-beasts among them.
- **Wolves, boars and bears** are the land's own animals, sharing the wild with broods that got out. Cold-blooded things don't last the winter, so the snow is still mostly the wolves'.
- **Cults** of the land's own powers, fire in the badlands and frost on the snow, hold the old stones and fight whoever comes, the Reich or a Defender.

**The Haven's giants** are the one kind of dinosaur that's none of these. Nobody knows how long they've been there: the Reich has never found the Haven, so it has never found them. There are three, the same three every time anyone comes home, and they're animals and nothing more, gentle because nothing has ever given them a reason not to be. They eat from the tops of the trees, call to each other with their mouths shut, as the bones say the great ones did, take a long look at anyone who comes near, and step carefully round whoever is under them. Nothing about them is the Reich's: no harness, no war paint, no black iron, oxblood or brass, and none of its beasts' voices, and a name that says one is harnessed, rotting or bones isn't grown at all. They're never in a land and never fought. They're also the first thing anyone sees, grazing beyond the hero behind the main menu.

## Writing it

- **The Reich is fictional, and coded on the Nazis without ever being them.** No real symbol, or anything shaped like one: no swastika, no runes, no death's head and no eagle. No real slogans, salutes, songs, people, units, places or events. Ranks are plain German army words and never the party's or the SS's, so nothing ending in "-führer". Its mark is its own, and must never look like any real flag.
- **The dinosaurs aren't the villains.** They're animals made into weapons. A wild one hunts, calls and runs as an animal does, and a harnessed one has had something done to it. The evil is the Reich's, so the writing never gives a dinosaur a will of its own: a tyrant's name is what its commander called it.
- **Nothing is taken from Jurassic Park.** No names, places, lines, logos or designs from the books or the films: the spitter has crests and a throat pouch and never a frill, and the Institute isn't a company. What's borrowed is a feeling: the ground shaking before the tyrant comes into view, a raptor calling in the long grass, pens and fences, and the wonder of great gentle beasts eating from the tops of the trees. Ghost of Tsushima is the same: its wind and its shrines are ideas, and ours are our own.
- **Plain words, and few of them.** A player reads a line while something is trying to eat them. Short sentences said the way a person would say them, as all the game's text is. People speak plainly and don't explain the world to each other.
- **The land's names are its own**, from `PlaceNames`, and the Reich's are in its tongue, which sounds German: commanders, broods and tyrants. Nothing is named halfway between.
- **These words mean something**, so use them and don't invent others for the same thing: the Saurian Reich (or the Reich), the Amber Institute, blood-doctors, the Ossuary, a brood, a war-band, a pen, greycoats, a tyrant, a Defender.
- **A new enemy says whose it is**: the Reich's (harnessed, in oxblood), the Ossuary's (bones or rot), escaped (wild colours, no harness), or the land's own. That decides its look, its name, its voice and where it's met.

## Where it stands

**Nobody has played this, read it in the game, or watched a dinosaur move anywhere but in the lab's pictures**, and the giants only in stills and short films taken by machine. Ask how it reads first.

Said in the game now: each land's chronicle, what places are, what villagers say, every enemy's name and look, and the dinosaurs' voices.

Not built:

- **A story across the lands.** Each land has a chronicle of its own, and nothing ties them into a campaign: no first landing, no Reich homeland, no end to the climb, and no leader of the Reich.
- **The Reich in the world's buildings.** Its pens are still the outlaw camps they were, with tents, and its keeps fly no pennant: `CampStage` and the site content know nothing of it. A pen should have cages and fences, and a keep its mark.
- **Music of its own.** The score doesn't change for the Reich.
- **A first screen that says any of this.** The menu shows the title, a hero standing in a world and the Haven's giants grazing beyond them, with nothing of the invasion.
- **Who a Defender is**, beyond the name the people give them.
- **Who lives in the Haven**, and how it's stayed hidden. It's a village of people who got out, and nothing more is said.

## Decided, and open to change

Choices made without asking. Say if any are wrong.

- **The name, the Saurian Reich.** "Reich" says at once who they're coded on, and "Saurian" what they fight with.
- **The mark**, a tyrant's skull in brass on oxblood. A claw was tried first and read as a real flag.
- **Black iron, oxblood and brass** for the Reich, cyan-green for the Ossuary's bones and yellow-green for its rot.
- **German-sounding names** for commanders, broods and tyrants, with plain army ranks.
- **Two orders**, the Amber Institute for the living and the Ossuary for the dead, so that skeletons and zombies have a reason to be there.
- **Every land's boss is a tyrant**, seven in ten of them bred and the rest bones or rotting. The human warlord who used to hold every land is nobody's boss now, though the kind is still there.
- **Commanders are never met.** The tyrant is the face of a land, as the dinosaurs are the point.
- **Outlaws are turncoats**, about a quarter of what holds the roads.
- **Broods that got out** are two in five of the beasts in the wild, with swarms of raptors and skitters another one in five. In the snow they're one in six, and the rest is still the wolves'.
- **Dinosaurs roar like film monsters**, not with the closed-mouth booming the evidence suggests, because these are bred and wrong. See `SOUND.md`.
- **The Haven's giants are free**, the only great beasts the Reich never found, as it never found the Haven, and there are three. They're after Brachiosaurus and Giraffatitan, and they boom with their mouths shut, as they aren't bred and wrong.
- **A raptor is a little slower than a hero**, 9.6 m/s against 10, so a pack can be got away from by someone who stops fighting.
- **The Haven is an isle of blossom in a golden evening**, peaceful and warm, with no black iron, oxblood or brass anywhere in it but the orders a Defender brings back.
- **Orders' names** are the Reich's plain army words and its tongue: a Kommandant, a Brutmeister, a garrison and a writ.

## Where this came from

- Michael Crichton's *Jurassic Park* (1990) and Steven Spielberg's film (1993): the premise of animals grown from blood in amber, pens and fences, a tyrant felt before it's seen, raptors that hunt together, and the spitter's venom, which is the film's invention and not the fossil's. Nothing else is used.
- Sucker Punch's *Ghost of Tsushima* (2020): an invasion from over the sea, a land that's what's being fought for, the guiding wind, shrines, and the people giving the one who fights for them a name.
- Tobias Riede and others, "Coos, booms, and hoots: the evolution of closed-mouth vocal behavior in birds", *Evolution* (2016): why the great ones most likely boomed with their mouths shut, which is how the giants call.
