# Places with a story of their own

Status: built on 2026-11-14 to the churchyard's standard (`CHURCHYARD.md`), and on the same day raised a second time, with the land's shrines, rings of standing stones, watchtowers and the Reich's pens rebuilt as places of their own and every place given worn ways, work clutter where its work is done, what lies underfoot, more light and a scene or two of what happened there. Every place below has been grown in 40 lands by `Dino WorldLab places` and `check` (407 places holding up, every land of two runs of 40 passing `check`, and `Dino NetLab places` and `lands` passing; a third run found a track under a pond's water in one frostmark land, which fails the same way on the commit before this work), and seen in stills from the game's own camera, every kind in one land or more and the wayside four in two or three each. **Nobody has walked through any of them, fought in one or watched a beast leave a cage**, and the second pass hasn't been freed on a running server by `Dino NetLab places` beyond the run noted under "Where it stands". Ask how they read first.

## The standard

The churchyard set it: a place that tells something, laid out as such places really are, and never the same twice. Each place here holds to the same rules.

- **It says whose it is and what happened there.** The Reich's places are black iron, oxblood and brass and nothing else of a land's is (`LORE.md`); the land's are its own way of building (`WorldData.StyleOf`) and its own colours; the Ossuary's are bones and its cold green light; what got out of the pens is wild-coloured and unharnessed; a cult's are its own power's, fire, frost, storm or mire.
- **It stands where it would.** A wreck lies on a beach with its stern in the water, a fishing village faces the sea and runs its jetty out to where a boat floats, a logging camp stands at the edge of woods worth felling (never bamboo or palms, `PlaceStory.IsTimber`), a quarry is cut into a hillside that climbs north, the way the camera looks, a nest is deep in the woods, a snow castle only stands in snow. `SiteDefinition` holds what a kind needs of the ground (`Climate`, `LeastWoods`, `WoodsNearby`, `Fells`, `LeastRise`, `MostAboveSea`), and `SiteStage.PlaceStories` puts down only kinds the land has ground for.
- **It's grown, never stored.** Every piece is a `PlaceSpec` id, `place:<piece>:<seed hex>:<numbers>` with no dots or hash, which `PlaceForge.Make` grows on worker threads (`Modelling/Procedural/Architecture/Places/`, headless). What a piece needs to know of its land comes in its id: the bark of the wood round a logging camp, the colour of the land's stone in a quarry, a shrine or a ring of stones (`PlaceMaterials.StoneOf`, from the `Geology` the cliffs are drawn in), the waygates' stone's shade in ruins.
- **It's laid out, not scattered.** A builder per kind (`World/Generation/Places/`, on `PlaceBuilder`) works out the shape first and then what goes in it, with room to fight, and everything tried and refused if the ground or what's there won't take it (`Tries`, `Put`, `Ground`, `Clear`). What it can't do without has a fallback that still tells the story: a dig the ground won't take a pit for is a shallow scrape with its beast coming out of it, a wreck with no room for her whole is her bow alone in the shallows, a beacon tower that won't stand to the north of its yard stands wherever in it it will, smaller if it must, a pen's kennels go along a side if its back won't take them, and a trader always has somewhere to stand (`StandNear`).
- **It's worked, and it shows where.** A place's people wear ways between what they use (`PlaceBuilder.Trail`): paved from a shrine's gateway to its altar, packed gravel from a laboratory's gate to its door and along a prison's cages, trodden earth down a logging camp's skid road and from a farm's yard out to every paddock's gate, a pale ring worn inside a ring of stones, a snow castle's snow trodden through to the earth from its gate to its altar. What's done somewhere drops something there (`PlaceBuilder.Strew`, `LitterKind`): chips round a sawpit, spalls round a banker, straw before kennels and cages, bones round a nest and a butcher's block, shells along a tide line, papers by a ledger, arms where a fight was, blood where something was killed or butchered, which shows on snow from anywhere. It's laid last, under and round everything, and walked through.
- **It's held, and the holders live there.** Whoever holds a place has a corner of it to live in, by its gate or its fire (`Camp.cs`): a brazier, a table with its bowls and lantern, a woodpile with its ends to the camera, a rack of their arms, a tent or a brood's command tent with its maps and standard, a wagon to take what's taken, cressets of the Reich's or a cult's torches, a barricade across the way in. The land's own tools are racked where its people left them.
- **It shows what happened.** Every place has a scene or two that says it without a word: the Reich's notice nailed over a shrine's name and its lanterns knocked down, a watchtower keepers' cart gone through on the way up, a nesting ground's edge where the Reich's hunting party came for what got out and didn't leave, a wreck's crew's last stand by the burst cages and the sailcloth some lived under after, a ring's barrow dug into by the Ossuary.
- **It's seen from 33 m up at 67°, looking north.** Tall things stand on a place's north side, a hatching shed is open towards the camera, a quarry's face faces it, ruins' back wall stands highest and their front lowest, a shrine's hall and a pen's kennels stand to the north facing it, a ring of stones is tallest to the north, a beacon tower stands at the back of its yard, a paddock's fence is low enough that what's in it shows, and flags strung on a line are lifted by the wind so their faces show whichever way the line runs. Roofs and boards are pale, as a dark one is a hole from up there.
- **It's freed, and something comes of it.** Every place a land asks to be freed has a chest of what its holders took. Freeing a prison or a farm swings every door and gate open and its beasts walk out into the wild (`CaptiveSystem`); the people of a logging camp, a quarry, a fishing village, a farm or a trading post come back to it (`SpawnRole.Returning`); a trading post's trader trades again (`SpawnRole.Trader`, a real merchant); and a shrine or a ring of stones can be kindled at its altar.
- **It's on the map**, in the chart's own hand: its buildings on their footprints, the Reich's in its dark stone, and its own marks (`PlacePlan.Marks`, drawn by `PlaceGlyph`): a wreck's hull, a pit or a quarry's floor hatched in from its edge, a ring's barrow, a nest a ring of twigs with its clutch, a web a few threads.

## How a place is made

- **Where:** `SiteStage.PlaceStories` runs once the ruins are placed, drawing from `SiteCatalog.Stories` by `StoryWeight` among the kinds the land doesn't have and has ground for, on a fork of its own. Quarries and digs are weighted down, as they need least of the ground. Shrines, rings of stones, watchtowers and pens are in `SiteCatalog.All`, as every land has some, and are laid out by builders of their own (`ShrineBuilder`, `StonesBuilder`, `TowerBuilder`, `PenBuilder`); `SiteDefinition.IsStory` counts them with the rest. They were given more room than they had for their forecourts, avenues and yards (a shrine 16 to 20 m, a ring 20 to 25 m, a watchtower 15 to 18 m), and a land too crowded for the one it must have looks again at three quarters of its smallest (`SiteDefinition.Squeezed`, `Squeezes`), its builder drawing everything in to fit, so that 38 lands in 40 have a ring of stones and 120 shrines stand in them, against 39 and 120 before. Roads stop short of what they'd cut through: at a walled place's gate or a quarry's mouth (0.92 of its radius), at a pen's gate (0.86), a shrine's gateway (0.85), the end of a ring's avenue or a watchtower's yard (0.88), and short of a dig, ruins, a snow castle and a nesting ground (0.85); passing roads go round a quarry (`RoadStage.KeepCore`).
- **The ground:** a place that reshapes the land does it with `GroundPad`s, applied in order with each over the one before (`TerrainBakeStage.ApplyPads`). A quarry lays the ground behind its face to its lip, the floor to its foot, and under its benches a ramp half a metre below the foot of every riser, so that the drawn ground, 0.15 m over where people stand, never comes up through a tread, and narrows its face until neither it nor its rubble is on a road. A dig steps down to a bench and a floor with a ramp up. A quarry's floor is a ground paint of its own, `GroundPaint.Chippings`, the land's stone broken small with the bedrock bare through it, which nothing grows through. Everything else is the paints there are, used for what they mean: paving where it's laid (a shrine's forecourt, a ruin's old floor), gravel where it's kept (a laboratory's or a pen's yard, the ways it wants kept), trodden earth where feet have been, mud where beasts are, ash round a fire cult's fire, a churchyard's dark turf inside a ring of stones and a garden's flowers under a shrine's old tree.
- **What stands:** `PlaceBuilder.Put` puts a piece down only if the ground and what's there let it (including trees), solid to its height, so that a blow is stopped by anything over 1 m. A wall's stretches stop short of their ends so they never touch, with no gap anyone fits through. Anything that rings a place has to leave a body room to get in from any side: the passable map is flooded from the landing round every solid grown by a body's radius (`FinalizeStage.BuildPassable`, 1.1 m), so a ring of stones too wide for its gaps shuts its altar in and the altar's prayer spot is moved out past it, which `check` fails. A standing stone is never wider than its share of the ring less 3.4 m, and stands on its slab's box, not the circle round it.
- **What lies flat:** `PlaceBuilder.Strew` lays litter (`PlacePiece.Litter`, `Underfoot.Litter`) with no footprint, last, smaller where the ground slopes so its edge doesn't float, and never on a road; `LayFlat` lays puddles, duckboards and nets the same way. `Lay` is for what lies low and mustn't have anything put on it.
- **What's held:** a caged or penned beast is a `PlaceCage` (`PlacePlan.Cages`), its door model, hinge and swing, where it stands, the way out and where it goes; a paddock is a `PlacePaddock`. `EncounterSystem` stands them and hangs the doors while the place is held, and `CaptiveSystem` swings the doors and walks the beasts out once it's freed, a penned one wandering its paddock until then. They're not anyone's to fight and nothing in the rules reads them.
- **What's kindled:** a shrine's and a ring's altar is the catalog's (`altar`, built by `Shrine.Altar`): a carved octagonal plinth with a bowl of old bronze on it and its embers banked and glowing, which is every altar in a land, villages' included. `WorldValidator` fails an altar spawn with no altar within 8 m.
- **The pieces** are in files by what they're for: `Shrine.cs` (the hall, the gateway, lanterns, offerings, the basin, the bell, ribbons, cairns, flags on a line and the altar), `Stones.cs` (standing stones lofted from rings of the land's rock, a lintel on two, the barrow, the cults' totems and hearts), `Beacon.cs` (the beacon tower, its wood under the tarp, the sighting stone, the horn), `Pen.cs` (kennels, the butcher's block, bite dummies, the midden), `Camp.cs` (racks of arms, torches of five kinds, braziers, woodpiles, wagons, the gibbet, barricades, the command tent, signposts, a mess table), `Works.cs` (the Institute's boiler, haystacks, a net spread to mend, a gutting table, a felling's slash) and `Underfoot.cs` (litter of twelve kinds, puddles, duckboards), beside each place's own.
- **New materials:** riveted plate, cut stone, netting and packed snow (`MaterialCatalog`, `MaterialRecipes.Places.cs`). Everything since is drawn in the materials there were, coloured in its vertices.

## Where it stands

**Nobody has walked through, fought in or freed any of these but the bots, the labs and stills from the game's camera.** Ask how they read first: whether each says at a glance what it is and whose, whether the quarry's face reads as a quarry and not a wall, whether a weaver's lair reads in its gloom, whether a ring of stones reads as a ring from up here, whether a pen under a dark sky is too dark, whether flags on a line read as flags, whether the beasts leaving a prison or a farm is a moment worth having, whether places turn up too often or too seldom, and whether the farm's grazers and the prison's captives are too small to see, and whether blood in the snow round a snow castle's kill and before its altar is too much.

Seen in the second pass, in stills from the game's camera: the shrine, ring of stones, watchtower and pen in two or three lands each, and every other place in one. The stills caught what the labs couldn't: a quarry's work refused because its floor is kept clear of what's put down as other places put things (`Keep`, so it's put down with `Floor` now), a ruin's tree whose crown hid the whole floor (it grows against the back wall now), a weaver's lair whose webs a camera this high doesn't see (it has a floor of silk now), a ring of stones on a beach that couldn't stand all its stones (it moves inland now), and a snow castle saying nothing white on white (it has trodden ways, blood and the cult's violet now). The fishing village, seen under rain, reads least changed from the camera, and is worth a look in good weather.

Not built:

- **Caves and mines.** A quarry is the only place cut into the ground.
- **A trading post's own goods.** Its trader trades as a village's merchant does.
- **Freed beasts staying anywhere.** They walk off and are gone; nothing of them is met again.
- **A wreck's sea.** Her stern stands in the water, and nothing moves her.
- **Night in any of them but by the land's own hours.** Only a churchyard is always at night.
- **A kindled altar looking kindled.** Its embers glow the same before and after.
- **Lanterns a shrine's freed lighting again.** They stay out.

## Decided, and open to change

Choices made without asking. Say if any are wrong.

- **The name**, places with a story of their own, or places, which is what the game already called them.
- **A prison's captives and a farm's grazers are herbivores caught in the wild**, hornbacks, clubtails, spikebacks, domeheads and striders in the wild's colours, so that freeing them frees animals and not the Reich's weapons. A prison holds four to seven of them.
- **A farm's paddocks are the farm's own fences**, low, with the Reich's black chain on their gates, as the Reich's tall pen palisade hid what was in them from the camera.
- **A snow castle is the north's children's, kept by the frost's cult**, its flame a pale violet that's nobody else's, as green is the Ossuary's and blue a ghost's.
- **Ruins are the waygates' builders'**, in a land gate's stone, so the two read as one people's work.
- **A trading post is the turncoats'**, the outlaws who sell to the Reich, and its trader comes back once they're gone.
- **A logging camp and a quarry are worked by the land's people for the Reich**, who come back to them freed.
- **The altar is a bowl of banked embers, warm**, where it was a floating green crystal: a shrine is kindled, as the lore says, and green is the Ossuary's.
- **A ring of stones' cult is its country's**, frost in the cold, mire in the wet, storm up high, fire where it's hot and dry and the place's story's choice elsewhere, and the four have a light each: flame, pale violet, lightning-white and a marsh-light's amber.
- **The stones hum in a faint warm white**, nobody's colour, where they glowed the blue a ghost is.
- **A watchtower's beacon stays cold**, and its sighting stone turns to the nearest other watchtower, or the keep if there's none.
- **A pen's kennels are the land's grey boards in the Reich's iron**, pale enough to read from up here, and its floor packed gravel, so that its ring and its butchery are dark mud against it.
- **A nesting ground has the Reich's failed hunting party at its edge** three times in four.
- **The sites are bigger**: a shrine 16 to 20 m, a ring of stones 20 to 25 m, a watchtower 15 to 18 m and a trading post 34 to 39 m, which moves where they and what comes after them stand in every land.
